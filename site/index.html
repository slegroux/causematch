How it works

Two sides, one yes each

A match takes two likes: the volunteer's, then the nonprofit's.

For volunteers

  1. Sign up with your phoneA text code, no password. Tell us where you'll help from, which causes you love, and when you're usually free.
  2. Swipe openings ranked for youEach card says why it's shown: a cause you love, a time you're free, a short trip. Like it or pass.
  3. Match, chat and show upWhen the nonprofit says yes, a full-screen "It's a match!" pops up on your phone, your first shift is booked and a chat opens. Likes still waiting stay on the Matches page. You get a text the day before.

For nonprofits

  1. Claim your organizationFind it by name in IRS records. A Causematch admin checks every request before anyone is added.
  2. Post openings anywhere in the countyWhat volunteers will do, where and when, how many people. Short of people this week? Post a last-minute call.
  3. Say yes, chat and track shiftsOne inbox shows who's waiting for an answer and which messages need a reply. Mark who came after each shift.
Confirmed volunteer hours

A record of the hours you gave

When a nonprofit marks you as attended, it confirms the hours of that shift in the same tap. Those hours are yours to keep.

Shifts and hours

Under Me, every hour a nonprofit confirmed, by month: the shift, the nonprofit and who confirmed it.

A certificate they can check

Under Shifts and hours, get a certificate per nonprofit: every confirmed shift, the total, a signature code, and a PDF with a QR code. Its check link works for anyone, signed out, and says whether it is genuine.

Nonprofits say so

A nonprofit that can confirm volunteer hours says it on its page, and its cards in your deck say "Confirms your hours".

Need them confirmed?

In your preferences, under Causes and places, tick "I need my hours confirmed" for school, court or a work program, and only nonprofits that confirm hours are shown.

Why it works

Built for the shift, not the sign-up sheet

Ranked, with reasons

Your causes, places and free times rank every opening. "Free in the next 48h" and filters narrow the deck when you want something now.

Last-minute calls

A nonprofit short of people in the next two days can text nearby volunteers who opted in and are free, with caps so nobody gets spammed.

Commitments that hold

Day-before "still coming?" texts, one-tap "can't make this one", and a warning when two shifts clash.

On a phone, no app store

It runs in the browser and can be added to your home screen in two taps.

Privacy by design

You decide what a nonprofit sees

  • A first name until they say yesA nonprofit sees "Sam L." and only the fields you agreed to share, until it accepts you.
  • Places, never your addressYou help from a neighborhood or ZIP code. Device location is rounded to about a kilometer.
  • Preferences stay privateYour places, how you rate each cause and your dealbreakers only rank your deck. A nonprofit sees a short summary after you like one of its openings.
  • Clean photos, quiet inboxPhotos are re-encoded with no hidden metadata, and sign-ins and texts are rate-limited.
